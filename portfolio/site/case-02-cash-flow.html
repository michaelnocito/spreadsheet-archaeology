Case study 02 · SMB services

The Money You Already Earned

A services business with a cash-flow problem and no cash-flow problem. The money was earned, invoiced, and sitting in other people's bank accounts.

  • Tableau
  • Python / pandas
  • AR aging & DSO
  • Synthetic data
  • ~2.5 hours build

This one uses synthetic data, and says so everywhere it appears. There is no public accounts-receivable dataset. The invoices are generated; the file shape is a faithful reproduction of a QuickBooks “Open Invoices” export. Presenting generated data as a real client's would be the fastest way to lose a client who checks.

The question

Who owes us, how late, and what is it costing us to wait? Every business that invoices asks this. Most answer it by scrolling a QuickBooks report, which is why most answer it wrong.

The trap: it's a report, not a table

QuickBooks doesn't export data. It exports a report, and that difference is where the money goes missing: 

Bright Fork Services LLC              ← company name
Open Invoices                        ← report name
As of July 29, 2026                  ← as-of line
                                      ← blank row
Num,Customer,Date,Due Date,Terms,...  ← the real header, line 5
10501,Blue Harbor Auto Group,06/03/2026,07/03/2026,Net 30,"1,599.64",...
10502,Blue Harbor Auto Group,05/18/2026,,Net 60,"3,033.81",...
     ,Total Blue Harbor Auto Group,,,,"5,465.49",...   ← subtotal INSIDE the data
                                      ← spacer row

Sum the Amount column as-is and you get roughly double the truth. Every invoice is counted once as a detail row and again inside its customer subtotal. The total looks plausible. It's just wrong — and nothing in the file tells you.

The decisions that mattered

The header is found, not assumed

The preamble is four rows here. It's a different number next month, and a different number again on a colleague's QuickBooks version. The pipeline scans for the row containing both Num and Customer. Hardcoding skiprows=4 works right up until it fails silently.

Scaffolding is removed by structure, not by keyword

One test does it: is the document number numeric? Real invoices have one; subtotals, spacers and the grand total don't. Pattern-matching on the word "Total" would also delete a customer legitimately named Total Comfort HVAC.

Only the open balance ages

On a partly-paid invoice, what's aging is what's still outstanding — not the original amount. Aging the full amount overstates old receivables and sends collections chasing money that already arrived. That call alone is the difference between a report people trust and one they quietly stop opening.

Missing due dates are derived, not guessed

Where the due date is blank but terms and invoice date are present, it's rebuilt as invoice_date + terms. That's recovery from a known business rule. Those rows are flagged, and the dashboard footer states how much of the aging rests on a derived date. Where terms are also missing, the row is excluded from aging rather than assigned a default.

The result

$194,786Open AR
113.7DSO (days)
$8,232Over 90 days
7 of 40Chronic late payers

218 lines in the export. 137 of them were actual invoices. The other 81 were the report talking to itself.

Dashboard: “The Money You Already Earned” Embed the Tableau Public viz here once published.

The recommendation. The over-90 balance isn't spread across the customer base — it concentrates in seven accounts out of forty. That turns an abstract cash-flow problem into seven phone calls, in a named order, with the invoice detail already assembled for each one. The dashboard's job is to produce the call list, not the chart.

The cost-of-float figure is driven by a parameter, not a hardcoded rate. I don't know a client's line-of-credit rate, and pretending otherwise puts a fabricated number on a page about money. Letting the owner move the slider themselves is also the moment they start trusting the rest of it.

Scope

About two and a half hours. Reruns against next month's export with one command and a pinned as-of date.
