Case study 01 · SMB retail

Where the Margin Went

A multi-store retailer knew its revenue. It did not know its margin, because the cost basis lived in a column that half the tooling read as text.

  • Tableau
  • Python / pandas
  • Star schema
  • ~3 hours build

Data: Iowa Liquor Sales — a real, public, transaction-grain feed published by the State of Iowa. Every defect described below is in the actual file.

The question

Not "how are we doing." The owner of a multi-site retailer asks one question every month and can't answer it: which products are taking up shelf space without earning it? Revenue reporting can't answer that, because the highest-revenue item and the highest-margin item are rarely the same thing.

The decisions that mattered

Returns are kept, not dropped

1.1% of lines have a negative quantity. They're returns. Filtering them out is the most common way a retail dashboard ends up overstating revenue — and it's invisible, because the resulting number still looks reasonable. They're retained, and carry an is_return flag so units-per-transaction analysis can exclude them deliberately.

Missing cost is excluded from margin, not imputed

67 lines have no cost basis. The tempting fix is to fill them with the item's average cost. That would smooth over exactly the pricing errors the dashboard exists to find. Instead those rows keep their revenue and carry a null margin, so Tableau excludes them from margin aggregates automatically.

Margin % is therefore computed on 98.3% of revenue, not 100%. That caveat is printed on the dashboard, not buried in a footnote. A number whose coverage you can't state isn't a number you should act on.

Margin % is an aggregate, never an average of percentages

AVG(margin_pct) weights a $6 bottle the same as a $600 case. The dashboard uses SUM(gross_margin) / SUM(sale_dollars). On this dataset the two differ by several points — enough to move an item across the cut threshold.

The recommendation. One product line — slow-moving, 10% margin, and missing a category in the POS entirely — accounts for a meaningful share of shelf space and almost none of the profit. It sits alone in the bottom-left quadrant of the scatter: slow and thin. Cutting it frees shelf for the top-quartile items without touching revenue in any way the owner would feel.

That the item was also uncategorized is not a coincidence. Products nobody has classified are products nobody is managing.

Scope

About three hours end to end: one to build the pipeline, two in Tableau. Rerunning it against next month's export is a single command, which is the part that makes this worth paying for once rather than every month.
